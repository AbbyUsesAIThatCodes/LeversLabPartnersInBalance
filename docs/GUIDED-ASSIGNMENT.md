## Force Semantics

The hanging weight represents the LOAD. The EFFORT is a stylized dark-skinned hand
and forearm pressing downward on the beam, with a constant-size hand and visible
beam contact at rest and during motion. Do not label the hand with mass.

Use an effort control labeled Effort Push (g-equivalent), with an automatically
computed Newton readout. A 100 g-equivalent setting means a downward push equal to
the weight of a 100 g object under the model's gravity: 0.981 N. The introduction
teaches this comparison; students are not required to perform a new force conversion.
Legacy packet effort grams map numerically 1:1 to this calibrated force setting.
The visible teaching, diagrams, tables, tooltips, and reports must explain that
these are equivalent-force settings, not the mass of a hand.

F_load = loadMass / 1000 * 9.81 N.
F_effort = effortEquivalentGrams / 1000 * 9.81 N.
At level, balance is F_load * loadArm = F_effort * effortArm.
Thus every existing packet numerical mass-arm equality remains unchanged, with
load grams compared against effort g-equivalent. These products are comparative
values, not SI torque; the model also computes actual N m internally.

Retain the legacy serialized effortMass key only for existing notebook compatibility,
with an explicit semantic adapter and versioned representation metadata. New student
surfaces and exports name the quantity correctly. Preserve old evidence as recorded,
and identify the representation revision rather than silently relabeling historical
hanging-effort observations. The applied force contributes torque, not fictitious
hand mass or force-dependent hand size. Update illustrative rotational inertia to
use the actual load and pointer; revalidate settling behavior as well as exact balance.

Role identities and coordinates remain separate. Swap moves application coordinates
without exchanging the load and effort roles. All packet limits, off-center arms,
Q7-to-Q11 trial linkage, IMA reciprocal comparisons, and Q13/Q14 constraints remain.
