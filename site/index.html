  1. Set Up: All controls stay usable. The beam pauses while you change load mass, effort push, or distances. Drag sideways to move a part; drag down to increase its setting, or up to decrease it.
  2. Predict And Test: Record a prediction to begin the test automatically. After changing the arrangement, record a new prediction. Earlier predictions and trials stay in your work.
  3. Observe: Wait for the recorded result. A moving beam passing through level is not yet balanced. Write what you actually saw.

Load And Effort: Gravity pulls the hanging load downward. The constant-size hand supplies a downward push. A setting of 100 g-equivalent means a force equal to the weight of a 100 g object (0.981 N), not the mass of the hand. No manual conversion is required.

Keyboard: Tab to a part label. Left/right arrows move it; down/up increases/decreases load mass or effort push. Number boxes, sliders, and halve/double buttons are also available. Drag the background to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom.

Swap Positions: Exchanges application coordinates while keeping load mass, effort push, and roles. Swap twice to return. The fulcrum stays between both points, with arms at least 75 mm. Coordinates and settings snap to 25-unit steps.

Ideal Model: Both applied forces point down through the labeled application points. The massless beam, cord, and pointer rod turn on an ideal pivot. A 250 g balance-pointer bob 125 mm below the axle supplies restoring torque when tilted. The hand supplies force without adding modeled mass or inertia. Motion uses illustrative damping and 12-degree stops, not a calibrated acceleration simulation.

Balance And Advantage: Compare load grams times load arm with effort g-equivalent times effort arm; these products are not SI torque. IMA is effort arm divided by load arm. At ideal balance only, load weight divided by required effort force equals IMA.
